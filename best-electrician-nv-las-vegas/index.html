What to check before you call

Start with a clear description of the issue or change you want. A contractor who handles a range of residential work may be able to discuss wiring, outlets, switches, lighting, surge protection, or an electrical panel upgrade. For a business, ask about commercial tenant improvements and explain what the space will be used for.

A few points to confirm during your first conversation:
- Does the company take on your type of work?
- Can you describe the location and what you have noticed?
- Will the proposed work and estimate be discussed before scheduling?
- Are there other people who need to approve the project, such as a landlord or property manager?

Big Red Electric Company reviews the job, explains the proposed work, and provides an estimate before scheduling. That gives you a chance to understand the planned scope and raise questions before deciding how to proceed.

Give a useful description of the problem

For a repair, note which room or equipment is affected, when the issue began, and whether it happens every time or only sometimes. If you have noticed a burning smell, unusual warmth, sparks, or repeated breaker trips, share that information when you call. Don't touch exposed wiring or attempt a repair yourself. If you think there is immediate danger, keep clear of the affected area and contact the appropriate emergency service.

For planned work, say what you want to add or change. A Level 2 EV charger, new lighting, additional outlets, or a panel upgrade each calls for different details. Photos can help explain the layout, but don't remove covers or open electrical equipment to take them.

Avoid these hiring mistakes

Don't choose based only on a quick answer or a broad promise. Ask what the visit or project review includes, what work is being proposed, and whether the estimate covers the items you discussed. If the explanation is unclear, ask for it in everyday terms before agreeing to schedule.

Also avoid starting a project before you know who can approve it. Renters should check with the property owner, and commercial tenants may need to coordinate with a building manager. This can prevent delays after the electrical plan has been discussed.

Get ready to schedule

Write down your questions, gather any relevant project notes, and make sure someone can provide access to the affected area. If you're comparing a best electrician , nv Las Vegas for a larger job, share the same description with each company so you can compare the proposed work fairly.
