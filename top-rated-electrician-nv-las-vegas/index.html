A short checklist before you call

It helps to gather a few facts before reaching out. You don’t need to diagnose the issue yourself. Describe what you’ve noticed and let the electrician assess the work.

  • Write down which room, circuit or equipment is involved.
  • Note when the issue began and whether it happens repeatedly.
  • Take a clear photo of the affected area if it’s safe to do so.
  • For a planned project, list the equipment or changes you have in mind.
  • Ask what information the contractor needs to understand the request.

When comparing electricians, ask who will review the work, what the proposed scope includes and how you’ll receive the estimate. For a larger project, ask if the estimate covers related work such as a panel or subpanel upgrade, or only the specific installation you requested. Clear scope helps you compare proposals fairly.

Questions worth asking

A few direct questions can make the first conversation more useful. Ask how the electrician plans to assess the issue and what factors could affect the work. If you’re considering an EV charger, ask what details about the charger and the home’s electrical system are needed before installation can be planned.

For lighting or wiring work, explain what you want to change and where. If this is a commercial project, describe the space and the tenant improvement work you have in mind. Avoid assumptions and know when to call

Don’t assume a recurring breaker trip, a warm outlet or flickering lights will resolve on its own. Avoid opening electrical panels or attempting wiring repairs if you’re not trained to do that work. If you notice burning smells, sparks or exposed wiring, keep people away from the area and contact an electrician promptly. If there is immediate danger, use local emergency services.

Another common mistake is describing only the symptom while leaving out relevant context. Mention recent appliance changes, renovation plans or other electrical work if they relate to the issue.
